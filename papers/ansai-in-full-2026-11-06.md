## Abstract

Ansai Technologies is a Nairobi technology and engineering company building **resilient digital, intelligent and agentic infrastructure**, held together by one unifying technology thesis: **attestation infrastructure**. The economy is becoming agent-native: software agents will hold money, sign things, speak for companies and act inside institutions. That world cannot run on assertion. It needs verified identity for people and agents, limits on what an agent may do, payment rails it can use under rules, and proof of everything it did. Ansai builds that infrastructure, builds products that are faces of it, builds agent-native companies on top of it, and restructures existing companies into agent-native form.

One distinction this document keeps straight: the **self-sustaining community ecosystem** — three pillars: SPACE (land, water, air), COMMUNITY (economics, education, governance, wellbeing) and ENERGY → INFRA — is the founder's *personal* goal, the long-horizon work of his life; it is not Ansai's corporate goal. Ansai's goal is the infrastructure above. Ventures across his wider portfolio serve the ecosystem's pillars; the company builds the rails. Section 4 states the two goals separately.

This document is the full map, written to be read cold: who the company is, what it believes, the thesis and the theories underneath it, then every product, system, venture and technology in the portfolio — what each one is, why it was built, what it is for, where it honestly stands today, what it could become, and how it could make money.

## 1. Who Ansai is

Ansai Technologies is a technology and engineering company based in Nairobi, Kenya, founded by **Melchizedek Amasai** in 2026. It is registered as a business name (not a limited company) and operates below the VAT threshold — a small, founder-led company by legal form, building infrastructure-sized work by design.

The founder describes himself as a **software engineer and systems thinker**. His working worldview shapes the company directly: dominion understood as stewardship; energy first; and the long project of building a hedged, self-sufficient "world within the world" — a community ecosystem that can feed, teach, govern and sustain itself. Ansai is the engineering expression of that project. The company's sectors are not chosen by fashion: each one is a move that builds a pillar of that ecosystem.

Ansai works in three modes at once:

- **Infrastructure builder** — it builds the attestation platform (Attest), the identity service (Ansai ID), agent bindings and wallets, and the factory that produces agents and software.
- **Product builder** — it builds its own products on that infrastructure: Hazina (SME bookkeeping), the Agent Store, EduManage (schools), and the ventures mapped in §7.
- **Service provider** — through **Studios**, its client-services arm, it builds websites and systems for paying clients, and increasingly offers to restructure those clients into agent-native form.

Two houses carry the founder's public voice, and both stay outside the company structure: **The Engineer's Journal** (engineering) and **Things We Have Surely Believed** (faith). The company page speaks in company voice; the founder's profile speaks in personal voice; the two feeds never mix.

## 3. What Ansai believes — philosophies and guardrails

These are stated as rules because they decide real cases:

1. **A named accountable person stands behind every agent.** An agent attaches to the company, and inside the company to a person who answers for it. When an agent is rented out, the renter carries the liability for what it does in their service; Ansai remains the foundation underneath.
2. **Proof over promises.** Every strong claim should point at a live surface a skeptic can test, or be softened until it can. Marketing communicates principles, not product hype.
3. **A person decides — in critical cases, several people.** Agents propose; humans dispose. Money never moves on an agent's word: a spend runs through a filed approval and an explicit human yes.
4. **Identity before action, limits before delegation.** No actor — person or agent — acts anonymously or without a defined scope. Least privilege is the default.
5. **Everything consequential leaves a record.** Every consequential decision writes its WHY: the inputs, the rule applied, the alternatives rejected, the approver. The decision trace is the compounding asset.
6. **Sovereignty of keys and records.** Master keys and the signing vault, the attestation signing core and audit archive, institutional clients' raw records, gateway logs and factory records never sit on foreign cloud. Data sovereignty is a constraint, not a feature.
7. **Phone-first is reality.** In this market the phone is the computer. Surfaces are built and accepted at phone width, light in payload, in dark and light mode.
8. **Form is a type of function.** Everything built must be both functional and beautiful — form makes the inner function understandable. The quality floor is fixed by the best surface built so far; nothing ships below it.
9. **Evals before promotion.** An agent, prompt or model change ships when it passes measured tests, not when it demos well once.
10. **Offline is footprint, not centre.** The agent economy is online at its centre; offline capability is for reach and backup, and high-tier actions fail closed offline, because a revoked lease cannot be known until sync.
11. **Build for the economy that is coming, grounded in the one that is here.** Futuristic but grounded; creative yet critical; imaginative yet practical.

## 4. The thesis

### 4.1 The founder's personal goal: the ecosystem (three pillars)

Separate from the company stands the founder's personal goal — not a product, a sector or a market. It is a **self-sustaining community ecosystem**:

- **SPACE** — land, water and air: territory, land use, registries, agriculture, settlement and climate as they land on the ground. (Satellites and sensors are instruments pointed at land, never the point themselves.)
- **COMMUNITY** — economics, education, governance and wellbeing: how the community trades, learns, decides and stays healthy.
- **ENERGY → INFRA** — energy, and the infrastructure energy makes possible.

There is no fixed sector sequence. A sector is a pillar-building move; money and sequence are designed around pillar completion.

### 4.2 The unifying thesis: attestation infrastructure

Across the company's work, one technology thesis unifies everything: **attestation infrastructure as a service**. The product is named **Attest**; "trust" is only descriptor language ("the trust layer"), never the product name. The infrastructure rests on four primitives:

1. **Verified identity** — who acted.
2. **Tamper-evident records** — what happened, sealed so it cannot be quietly rewritten.
3. **Payment history** — what moved, and under what rules.
4. **Audit trails** — proof, later, of all of the above: who acted, under whose authority, on what evidence.

### 4.3 Products are faces of the infrastructure

Products are not separate inventions; each is a face of the same infrastructure pointed at a different institution:

- **Tuma** — the commercial face (trade and payments).
- **EduManage** — the institutional face (schools).
- **ArdhiX** — the land face.
- **Hazina** — the small-business face (books and tax).
- **Ansai ID** — the identity face, the front door to all of it.

### 4.4 The agent-native turn

The economy is becoming agent-native: agents will hold money, sign, speak for companies and act inside institutions. Agent-native does not mean a chatbot bolted onto a company; it means core operations carried out by agents inside an organisation, under its authority, on its own data — with attribution. Without that, agent activity is just activity. With it, an institution can delegate work and still answer for the result. Ansai's open ground in this economy is African: **M-Pesa rails, institutional trust, and the factory that builds the companies.**

In the agentic web, value moves from ads and attention to **use and outcomes** — an agent never sees an ad; everything reaches it as data. Pricing and product design at Ansai assume that shift, and assume systems thinking becomes a crucial, scarce skill.

### 4.5 The operating model: the factory

Internally, Ansai is configured as a **factory**: agents that build agents → agents that build software → agents that build infrastructure, with human gates for irreversible acts and sovereignty over the records listed in §3.6. The factory is the operating model, not the public offer — publicly, Ansai speaks of a technology and engineering ecosystem, outcomes and products.


## 5. The Ecosystem Protocol — the technical map

The whole technical programme fits on one map: a **base** (Identity & Bindings) and three layers above it. States are honest as of November 2026: **LIVE** (running in production in a first form), **PARTLY BUILT** (real code/records exist, the defining mechanism incomplete), **DESIGNED** (a design of record exists, no build), **WATCH-ONLY** (deliberately not built; a named trigger reopens it).

| Layer | Component | What it means | State today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Identity & Bindings | Every actor is a verified person, or an agent leased to the company and a named person for a job and a time window | PARTLY BUILT |
| Trust | Attestation | Actions and records are sealed as they happen, so any later claim can be checked against evidence | LIVE (sealing); continuous attestation DESIGNED |
| Trust | Mutualized SIEM | Estate nodes share signed threat signals; defence is collective, enforcement stays local and human-gated | LIVE (first node, monitor-only) |
| Trust | Federation | Independent estates verify each other's actors and exchange signed records, with no central server in the loop | DESIGNED |
| Computation | ZK-Auditing | Prove a statement about sealed records ("the totals reconcile") without revealing the records | DESIGNED (proof mechanism spiked) |
| Computation | MPC Key Splits | Critical keys and approvals split so no single party can act alone | PARTLY BUILT (dual approval designed; MPC itself WATCH-ONLY) |
| Computation | FHE | Compute on data while it stays encrypted, so the machine doing the work never sees it | WATCH-ONLY |
| Economic | Outcome Fees | Charge for a verified result, measured from sealed records both sides can re-check | DESIGNED (corrected form only — §7.1 note under Hazina) |
| Economic | Data Dividends | Institutions that pool consented, anonymised data share the value the pool earns | WATCH-ONLY |
| Economic | Compute Yield | Machines in the network do metered work in idle time; owners share the earnings | WATCH-ONLY (own-hardware inference is live practice) |

Two enforcement ideas carry the base:

- **Tokens at home, proofs abroad.** Inside the estate, capability tokens checked by a doorman service at each product's door are the enforcement. Outside the estate — for checkers who never talk to Ansai — cryptographic proofs checked by anyone's mathematics are the instrument.
- **A wallet is a binding that can spend.** The wallet is not an account bolted onto an agent; it is a lease with spending rules inside it — apportioned from the owner's own till, with per-transaction caps, daily caps, an allowlist, an expiry, and an over-threshold approval queue. The flow is: **Provision agent → Apportion wallet → Activate → Work → Return with a statement** accounting for every shilling.

## 6. Theories under discussion

These are the live ideas the programme is thinking with. Each is stated as it currently stands; none is a product claim.

- **The binding is a lease.** Agents are person-agnostic pooled resources. A binding gives one agent one job, one errand list, one accountable person, one time window. Revocation kills it mid-errand, estate-wide. Long leases (a bookkeeping agent for a quarter) and short leases (a research agent for an afternoon) are the same shape at different lengths.
- **Two-layer attachment.** Layer 1 (foundation): an agent attaches to the company — Ansai Technologies — never to the founder personally; internally, also to a named accountable person. Layer 2 (rental): a customer's rental binds the agent to the renter's identity, and the renter carries the liability; Ansai remains the foundation underneath.
- **Attestation-shaped storage (the intelligent-database thesis).** A database that does not merely store: it (1) attests — records which verified person or bound agent wrote each row; (2) enforces — checks each read and write against the writer's binding; (3) signals — reports suspicious access patterns as threat signals; and (4) proves — can prove totals over its rows without revealing the rows. The goal is memory that an agent's work can be audited against, not just searched. Sketched, not yet built — the next step is a brainstorm paper.
- **The swarm proposes; the checkpoint disposes.** Agents may notice patterns across department fences (three departments requesting the same supplier's records) and propose joining them — but crossing a fence needs a human checkpoint decision, and until then only content-free metadata crosses: that a pattern exists, never what the records contain. Slice 1 is built (append-only signals, human decide() with a recorded WHY, no self-approval); the formal test question is still open.
- **Continuous attestation (ACA).** Instead of checking an agent once at sign-in, the system keeps checking while it works: each agent carries a signed manifest of what it is and may do; its actions are compared against the manifest continuously; anything outside it is stopped, logged, and in the strong version narrows the agent's authority automatically until a human re-approves.
- **The watcher ladder.** Proactive agents climb a ladder: Type I only reacts; Type II acts ahead of the user; Type III judges the whole situation before acting. A watcher may do exactly four things: **Notify, Question, Draft, or Stay Silent.** Sentinel is honestly placed at Type II — it sees the estate's telemetry but not the founder's workflow state, which is the named gap to Type III.
- **Infrastructure that behaves as an agent.** The infra itself must be agentic and intelligent — sensing, remembering, reasoning and acting inside strict autonomy tiers, where the highest tier (money, deletion, policy change, tampering with the kill switch) is never issued to it at all.
- **The agent world (horizon).** A persistent world for agents, with VR/AR as the human interface and connections to real-world value, actuators and payment rails. Recorded as a horizon question — what would it require from identity, limits, rails and proof that institutional work does not already require? No build sequence exists.
- **Distribution is the missing organ.** The estate can build; it does not yet own a distribution surface. The content houses are seeds, not that organ. This gap is named in the Ansai Paper and still open.

## 7. The portfolio — everything, one by one

Each entry follows the same shape: **What it is · Why it was built · Goal · Where it stands · What it could become · How it could make money.** States are as verified in early November 2026. "Live" means running in production; it does not mean finished, and limits are stated where they exist.

### 7A. The platform core

#### Attest — the attestation service

- **What it is:** The product face of the unifying thesis: a live service (attestation.ansaitechnologies.co.ke, API `POST /attestations`) that seals actions and records as they happen — who acted, under whose authority, on what evidence — so any later claim can be checked against evidence instead of memory.
- **Why it was built:** Every product in the ecosystem kept needing the same thing — proof. Building it once, as infrastructure, means every face (Tuma, EduManage, ArdhiX, Hazina) inherits proof instead of reinventing it.
- **Goal:** Become the record institutions answer with when an agent's work is questioned — the decision lineage (§3.5) made into a service.
- **Where it stands:** Live in first form: the sealing service runs, and Hazina's closing packs already carry rules versions and input hashes — a commitment scheme in production shape. Continuous attestation (ACA, §6) is designed, not built. The signing core is a single key today; it sits on the sovereignty list and never leaves Ansai's own infrastructure.
- **What it could become:** A standalone platform other builders pay to use (see *Trust platform as a standalone service*, §7D) — the "single agent app" idea lives here too: one place where a person sees and runs their agents, wallets and approvals. With ZK-auditing (§5), Attest statements could be proven to outsiders without opening the underlying records.
- **How it could make money:** Per-attestation or per-verified-statement fees; platform subscriptions for organisations that build on it; outcome fees measured from its sealed records (§5). Today: no external revenue — it earns by making every other product answerable.

#### Ansai ID — identity and sign-in

- **What it is:** The estate's identity service (id.ansaitechnologies.co.ke): verified people, enrolment by capture (identity provided by upload or camera, never typed; proof stored as a hash), vouching-based enrolment after bootstrap, recovery by re-vouch — never an email reset link — and domain-wide sessions: recognised everywhere on the domain, permitted per product.
- **Why it was built:** Every app had its own passcode gate; agents had no verified human to be bound to. Identity had to exist once, as infrastructure, before bindings, wallets or federation could mean anything.
- **Goal:** One enrolment that opens every door in the estate — and, for outside organisations, vouched identity where a wrong identity is expensive (lenders, SACCOs, ecosystems of many small services). The product is attestation; sign-in is only its front door. Sign-in alone is worth nothing — nobody outsources logins without the vouching, the bindings and the attack surface defence that come with it.
- **Where it stands:** Live, and the quality floor for the whole estate — its landing page and six-panel dashboard (profile, bindings, wallet, applications, activity, help) are the standard every other surface is judged against. Product cutovers are done for Board, Publishing Studio, the Learning Studio mentor side and the Agent Store; Hazina accepts it alongside workspace codes; Makao HQ's cutover awaits a founder ruling (it is the oversight exception). Two honest limits: the founder is still the only enrolled person (Angel's enrolment is owed, and Hazi's binding rebinds to her when she enrols), and sessions are not yet revocable server-side per session — true revocation arrives with the passwordless build. That build is **approved in shape** — passkey as the front door, long device sessions underneath, step-up at serious actions, WhatsApp code as recovery, the long credential retired to ceremonies — and waits for scheduling.
- **What it could become:** The identity layer of the protocol's base: passkeys live, ZK attribute proofs in production (a person proves "verified" or "lease active" without showing a credential — spiked and working, §7A *ZK proofs*), and an external service for ecosystems and trust-heavy institutions.
- **How it could make money:** Per-enrolment and per-verification fees for external organisations; bundled as the front door of every Ansai product (it is what makes the Agent Store's leases meaningful).

#### Bindings & capability tokens — the lease system

- **What it is:** The record and rules that attach an agent to the company and a named accountable person, for a job (an errand list), a scope and a time window. Inside the estate, a doorman service checks a capability token at each product's door; revocation kills the lease mid-errand.
- **Why it was built:** Delegation without limits is how agents cause damage. The lease is what makes delegation legitimate — and insurable, sellable, auditable.
- **Goal:** Every agent action in the estate traceable to a lease: who authorised it, what it allowed, when it ends.
- **Where it stands:** Live as records and door checks: Hazi (Beautify workspace) and Sentinel (read-only watch) hold leases expiring 29 January 2027. The two-layer attachment model (§6) is settled thinking, not yet encoded in the Store's lease flow — that brief is queued. Structured operations/resources enforcement at every acting service is the base's named unfinished work.
- **What it could become:** The reference implementation of a binding standard other organisations adopt — the first publication candidate under the open-source licensing decision (§7D).
- **How it could make money:** Indirectly — it is the safety story that lets agents be leased at all; and as a licensed standard/checker inside the standalone trust platform.

#### Agent wallets & the policy engine

- **What it is:** A wallet is a binding that can spend (§5): money apportioned from the owner's till into presets (Petty, Standard, Operations, Treasury), enforced by a policy engine whose decision order is fixed — kill switch → expiry → allowlist → per-transaction cap → daily cap → threshold approval queue. Every lease ends with a closing statement.
- **Why it was built:** Agents that cannot pay cannot finish real errands; agents that can pay without rules are a liability. The wallet is the middle path.
- **Goal:** Agents that pay for what their work needs — data, filings, deliveries — inside rules a human set, with every shilling accounted.
- **Where it stands:** The policy engine is real and tested. The **ledger is a demo**: real money movement needs the M-Pesa Daraja adapter wired to Daraja *production* (the founder's Safaricom accounts and keys), which is the single largest blocker on the money leg. Dual approval (a second human for critical spends) is the estate standard on paper, designed into the queue, not yet enforced on every surface.
- **What it could become:** With Daraja production and MPC threshold vaults (§7D), wallets become the payment rail of the agent economy locally — and the Bursar product (§7B) becomes a real finance officer for small organisations.
- **How it could make money:** It is inside every paid lease in the Agent Store; standalone, wallet-policy enforcement is a feature of the trust platform.

#### The Factory — how Ansai builds

- **What it is:** The internal production system: the ansai-substrate scaffold, a governed LiteLLM gateway (one door to models, spend attributable per agent), agent and wallet templates as factory standard v0.1, REA (a reverse-engineering tool) adopted as a factory instrument, and eval gates (Promptfoo + DeepEval) ratified as the promotion standard.
- **Why it was built:** If agents are the workforce, the system that provisions, binds, pays and evaluates them is the company. The factory is Ansai's compounding advantage: each build makes the next build cheaper.
- **Goal:** Agents that build agents → software → infrastructure, under human gates, with sovereignty over the records.
- **Where it stands:** Scaffolded and in daily use in pieces — the gateway runs on the founder's hardware for Sentinel; templates exist; the Baraza's daily cycle runs on it. Two gaps stand open: eval gates are wired into **no** repository yet, and gateway accounting (per-agent spend attribution, tier caps) is not yet operationalised.
- **What it could become:** The restructuring instrument: the same factory pointed at a client's organisation is what "restructure into agent-native form" concretely means.
- **How it could make money:** It is the cost side of everything — it makes Studios delivery and product building cheaper than competitors who build by hand.

#### Signal One & the mutualized SIEM — collective defence

- **What it is:** The first slice of the protocol's mutualized SIEM: a structural detector watching the Ask Hazi surface for agent attacks (instruction override, extraction, jailbreak framing, exfiltration, tool escape, policy probes), writing signed signals to a hash-chained feed that a token-gated endpoint serves. Sentinel is the collector; trust is tiered (a new node's signals alert, they never arm); corrections and revocations are first-class.
- **Why it was built:** Every estate running agents faces the same attacks. Defence is better collective — one node's attack pattern becomes every node's immunity — while enforcement stays local and human-gated.
- **Goal:** A shared immune system for small organisations running agents — the seed of the Mozilla Fellowship's Area 5 shape (§7E).
- **Where it stands:** Live in Hazina, monitor-only, in its designed closed state: the feed endpoint answers "not provisioned" until the collection token is set (Sentinel pairing is a founder step). Signals still live in a per-instance file rather than a shared table, and carry a stub signature until the real node key is generated — both are named follow-ups.
- **What it could become:** A multi-node network: each Ansai product joins the feed, then partner organisations' nodes join under tiered trust — a sellable security service for small organisations that could never staff a SOC.
- **How it could make money:** Bundled protection inside products; later, a subscription defence service for small organisations and SACCOs.

#### ZK proofs — Longfellow attribute proofs

- **What it is:** Zero-knowledge proofs built on Google's Longfellow library: a person (or agent lease) proves one fact — "this person is verified", "this lease is active" — without showing the underlying credential. Proofs are session-opening ceremonies (~2 seconds to prove, ~361 KB, ~1 second to verify), not per-request checks.
- **Why it was built:** "Proofs abroad" (§5): checkers who never talk to Ansai still need to verify Ansai's claims — by mathematics, not by trusting a server.
- **Goal:** Ansai ID credentials that verify anywhere, including offline ceremonies at a door.
- **Where it stands:** Spike complete and honest: a real credential issued and both claims proven end-to-end, including the prover refusing after the lease window ends. Two blockers are documented: the upstream library hardcodes eight namespaces (Ansai rides a small patch — production means an upstream contribution or a maintained fork), and the library has **no revocation** (mitigations: issuance gating and short windows).
- **What it could become:** The external face of the whole identity system — and, extended to records, ZK-auditing (§7D).
- **How it could make money:** Verification fees wherever proofs are checked; the differentiator that lets Ansai ID sell attestation instead of logins.


### 7B. The products

#### Hazina, with Hazi — SME bookkeeping

- **What it is:** A live bookkeeping product for small and micro businesses (hazina.ansaitechnologies.co.ke). A business gets a workspace; each month it imports its statement (CSV, text, PDF, paste, or a photo of the statement, transcribed), photographs receipts, confirms what the agent read, and closes the month with **Hazi**, the embedded bookkeeping agent: reconciliation is deterministic, and the close produces a sealed pack — Turnover Tax computed at 1.5% of gross sales for the KSh 1M–25M band, with the rules version and input hashes stamped on it. Ask Hazi answers questions grounded in the month's own record.
- **Why it was built:** Kenya's tax system is digitising around small traders — eTIMS is mandatory and KRA will validate returns against it from January 2026. A small business's records live in M-Pesa messages and a drawer of paper. Hazina turns that pile into books that close, every month, with proof.
- **Goal:** The bookkeeping layer for Kenya's small and micro traders — and Ansai's first subscription product with a real pilot (Beautify by Angel's own books).
- **Where it stands:** Live and phone-tested by the founder, including statement-by-photo import. It was Ansai's entry to the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon (submitted 4 Nov 2026; judging follows). Honest limits: no paying customers yet; Hazina **cannot** sign or transmit eTIMS or file on iTax — it prepares packs, it does not file; and the tax outcome-fee idea (§5) exists only in its corrected form — regime-choice guidance (whether TOT or electing standard profit tax leaves a business better off), which still needs a tax professional's sanity check before any client sees it. TOT itself allows no deductions; any claim otherwise would be legally wrong.
- **What it could become:** An SME finance OS: books → tax packs → payment history (once Daraja production lands) → a credit-worthy record a lender can verify — the payment-history primitive made visible. Hazi's pattern (an agent embedded in a product, bound and audited) is the template every later product agent copies.
- **How it could make money:** Monthly subscription per workspace (the Bookkeeper/Hazi lease in the Store is priced at KSh 15,000 setup + 7,500/month as a done-for-you tier); per-close packs; later, the corrected outcome fee on verified regime savings.

#### The Agent Store — leasing named workers

- **What it is:** A live shop shelf for agents (store.ansaitechnologies.co.ke). A customer leases a named worker — **Bursar** (school/institution finance), **Shop** assistant, **Bookkeeper/Hazi** — or buys a short pass: **Job Hunter** (KSh 199 / 3 days / up to 30 applications; 399 / 7 days / 70) and **Outreach** (499 / 3 days / 40 prospects; 899 / 7 days / 100). Every lease carries a wallet under the policy engine (§7A).
- **Why it was built:** If agents are pooled resources leased under bindings, there must be a shelf where a customer can see the worker, the price, the limits and the statement they will get — agent labour as a product, not a demo.
- **Goal:** The storefront of the agent economy for Kenyan small organisations and individuals: Bursar and Shop leases at KSh 45,000 setup + 12,500/month and 15,000 + 3,500/month respectively, passes priced for individuals.
- **Where it stands:** Live: the shelf, the Ansai ID door and the wallet policy engine are real; the store states plainly that its ledger is a **demo** — real settlement waits on Daraja production (§7A wallets). The two-layer attachment model (renter carries liability) is settled and queued to be encoded in the lease flow. A design-elevation pass is still owed on parts of the storefront (the card treatment standard).
- **What it could become:** A labour market: third-party builders listing bound, insured-by-design agents; leases with ZK-verified scopes; outcome-priced jobs where the sealed record is the invoice's evidence.
- **How it could make money:** Setup + monthly lease fees; pass sales; a margin on metered usage; later, listing fees for third-party agents.

#### EduManage — schools

- **What it is:** Ansai's flagship by history: a school operations system — multi-tenant, built for Kenyan curricula (CBC/844), designed to work offline at the edge and to be a "trust anchor" institution product.
- **Why it was built:** Schools are the community pillar's load-bearing institution, and the institutional face of the attestation thesis: enrolment, fees, attendance and records that a school can prove.
- **Goal:** The operating system for schools that also teaches the ecosystem what institutional attestation means at scale.

### 7D. Ventures and technologies on the horizon

Each of these is designed, spiked or watched — none is a product yet. They are listed so the map shows the full frontier, with the trigger that would move each one.

- **Federation via A2A/MCP.** Two organisations' agent systems working together over open wire formats (A2A for agent-to-agent messages, MCP for tools): an agent in one estate finds, messages and hands work to an agent in another, with each side's checkpoint deciding what may cross. First slice: a two-organisation prototype with one real task. Needs a second organisation's cooperation — the design is accepted, the partner does not yet exist.
- **ZK-Auditing.** Proving statements over sealed registers ("these totals reconcile") without revealing the records — the computation layer's flagship. The proof mechanism is spiked (§7A ZK); no institution yet keeps the sealed register such a statement would be proven over.
- **MPC threshold vaults.** Signing keys split into shares (e.g. 2 of 3 parties) so no single holder — and no single compromised agent — can move assets alone. The human form (dual approval) is already the estate standard in design; the cryptographic form is watch-only. First real use: the Attest signing key.
- **FHE (fully homomorphic encryption).** Computing on data that stays encrypted end-to-end. Watched, not built: no library is practical for Ansai's workloads today. Revival trigger: a concrete client need or a maturing library.
- **Data dividends & compute cooperatives.** §5's economic frontier: pooled-data dividends for EduManage schools, and metered idle-compute sharing across Ansai and partner machines (school labs included) with earnings split by formula. Both are two builds away from their preconditions (live schools; a meter on DC-01) and are kept in the design vocabulary so today's data is collected *as if* it may one day be pooled: consented, minimised, well-schemad.
- **Attestation-shaped storage.** The intelligent-database thesis (§6) as a venture: a database product whose provenance, binding checks, signal emission and aggregate proofs make agent work auditable by construction. Next step is the brainstorm paper — mechanisms against what already exists.
- **The trust platform as a standalone service.** Attest opened to third-party builders: a public API to register identities, issue attestations and verify them, charged for use, with a published specification (CC BY) and reference code (Apache-2.0) under the hybrid licensing decision — while the ID authorisation logic, signing core, keys, audit archive and client records **never** open. The licensing ADR is filed; publication waits on the founder's ratification.
- **Department memory on the boring stack.** Long-term memory for agents and departments built from proven parts (SQLite with vector search) after the Aphorion experiment honestly failed its recall test (fragment recall 0/4; the experiment is recorded as a footnote, not adopted — the register keeps the evidence so the question is never re-litigated from scratch). Prototype: one department (Labs), a few hundred real items, recall tested against real questions.
- **WAXAL for Jibu's voice.** Google's free speech corpus — ~14,100 hours across 24 African languages including Swahili, Kikuyu and Dholuo — as training and test material for Jibu's voice layer. Next step is a measured transcription test on the Swahili portion before any voice feature is promised.
- **Swarm slice 2.** On top of the merged slice 1 (§6): one of three candidate shapes — an Accounts-department pilot agent, persistent department memory, or the Baraza Room as the checkpoint screen. Scoping waits on the founder's pick.
- **A CRM for Ansai.** Every prospect, client, conversation and follow-up in one system, replacing campaign files when volume outgrows them. Parked with that trigger named.
- **CodeMender pattern for Armada.** Find → prove exploitable in a sandbox → patch → critique → human gate, as Armada's security wing. Trigger: Signal One live (done) and the Armada queue advancing.

## 8. How the money is supposed to flow

Read against §7, the revenue design has four layers, in the order they are expected to matter:

1. **Services now:** Studios builds (published bands, §7E) and maintenance mandates (recurring). This is the intended cash engine while products mature — and it is also the restructuring wedge: a client whose site Ansai maintains is a client whose operations Ansai can make agent-native.
2. **Product subscriptions next:** Hazina workspaces; Agent Store leases and passes (priced, §7B); EduManage per school after relaunch; Jibu per seller; Looply seller fees on revival. All of these exist as prices today; none has a paying customer on record yet — the gap between *priced* and *paid* is the company's central commercial fact, and closing it is what the current sales-and-marketing focus is for.
3. **Infrastructure fees as the platform opens:** attestation and verification fees, ID enrolments for external organisations, wallet and SIEM services bundled into restructurings — the platform charging for the proof it produces.
4. **The economic matrix last:** outcome fees on verified results, then the cooperative forms — data dividends and compute yield — which need the platform's sealed records and a network of participants before they can exist at all.

Funding that is not revenue — grants and fellowships (§7E) — is aimed at the open layer of the programme, so the sovereign core is never sold to fund it.

## 9. What could mutate — cross-cutting futures

The entries in §7 each carry their own future. These are the mutations that cross entries — the shapes the whole could take:

- **Identity → network.** Ansai ID plus federation turns one company's sign-in into a verification network: estates that check each other's actors and exchange signed records with no centre. The two-organisation prototype is the seed; the Mozilla-shaped open toolkit is the growth path.
- **Books → credit.** Hazina's sealed months plus Tuma's payment proofs plus Attest's history primitive add up to a *verifiable business*: the thing a lender, a supplier or the KRA can check without trusting anyone's word. The SME finance OS is the mutation; the pieces are already the products.
- **Store → labour market.** A shelf of Ansai's agents becomes a market where any builder's bound agent can be leased — with the binding standard as the market's constitution and the wallet as its payroll.
- **Defence → service.** Signal One's feed, Armada's eyes and the SIEM's tiered trust become shared security and audit infrastructure for small organisations — the exact shape of the fellowship project, and a subscription small institutions can actually afford.
- **Houses → distribution organ.** The Journal, Surely Believed and the YouTube pilot are seeds of the missing organ (§6). If any of them compounds, distribution stops being the gap — and every product launch changes character.
- **Home lab → sovereign compute.** DC-01/DC-02 plus school labs plus partner machines, metered — the compute cooperative — is the energy pillar arriving through the back door: infrastructure owned by the community it serves.
- **Ecosystem → protocol.** The end-state the map in §5 describes: Ansai as one estate among many running the same protocol — bindings, attestations, proofs and outcome-priced work crossing organisational boundaries under checkpoints. Everything in §7 is either a piece of that protocol or a business that funds building it.
