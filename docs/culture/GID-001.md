## 01 — Who we are

**Ansai Technologies is the operational infrastructure
platform for Africa.**

### Mission

We build software for the institutions that shape African
life — schools, farms, clinics, and the communities
they serve.

### What operational infrastructure means

Infrastructure is the foundational layer that everything
else runs on. Operational infrastructure is specifically
the systems that make the day-to-day running of an
institution possible — the things an institution needs
to function every single day, not occasionally.
Remove it and the institution cannot operate properly.

Ansai does not build apps people use when convenient.
We build the systems institutions depend on.

**The test:**
If an Ansai product went offline for a week, would the
institution it serves feel it in its daily operations?
If yes — that is infrastructure.
That is the standard we build to.

### The model

What M-PESA did for payments in Africa — becoming so
foundational that removing it would break how an entire
economy functions — Ansai builds for operations.

The ambition is not to be the largest software company
in Africa. It is to be the most depended-upon.

## 02 — The vision

Ansai Technologies is building the operational software
infrastructure for Africa. We enter every sector where
Africans need reliable, locally-built technology to
function with clarity and confidence — in schools,
on farms, in clinics, and in every institution that
shapes daily life.

We start in education because schools sit at the centre
of every African community. The trust earned inside a
school extends into the community that school serves.
From schools, we grow into agriculture, health, logistics,
digital economies, energy, and the cities where all of
these institutions converge. Not as separate bets, but
as a connected ecosystem where each product line makes
the others stronger.

Beneath every product line sits a shared enabling
technology stack — built once by Ubunifu Village and
deployed across all product lines. The product lines
earn the right to build this stack. The stack compounds
the value of every product line.

## 04 — The enabling stack

The sectors above are where Ansai serves institutions.
The layers below are what Ubunifu Village builds and
owns across all of them. They are not separate businesses.
They are Ansai's compounding technological advantage.
Built once, deployed everywhere.

Each product line that reaches scale earns the right
to deepen the next layer of this stack.
No layer is built before the product lines that
justify it exist. Sequence is discipline.

| Layer | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| AI and Intelligence | Decision support, prediction, and automation woven into every product line. The institutional data accumulated by each product line is what makes this intelligence layer genuinely African and genuinely useful. Ansai Ziwa is where this intelligence accumulates. |
| IoT and Sensing | Physical world data collection at the institutional level — soil sensors on farms, attendance hardware in schools, water flow meters in clinics. Bridges Ansai's software and the physical reality African institutions operate in. |
| Edge and Cloud Infrastructure | Compute that works with or without reliable connectivity. Africa's infrastructure reality demands software that runs at the edge — locally, on device, in the institution — and syncs intelligently when connectivity is available. This is Ansai's architectural advantage, not a constraint. |
| Chain and Trust | Distributed identity, non-repudiation, immutable agreement records. The infrastructure underneath every governance agreement between Ansai and the institutions it serves. |
| Robotics and Automation | Physical automation for agriculture, logistics, manufacturing, and institutional operations. As Ansai's sector depth grows, robotics becomes the physical execution layer for the software systems already built. |
| Quantum | Long horizon. Cryptography, supply chain optimisation, logistics modelling, and computational problems classical systems cannot solve at scale. Ansai positions early — not to build today, but to ensure African institutions are not dependent on foreign quantum infrastructure when it arrives. |

## 05 — Company structure

### Current structure

Ansai Technologies is one registered legal entity.
All product lines operate within this single entity.
Ubunifu Village is the R&D and infrastructure arm —
it has no separate legal existence.
It is Ansai's internal Bell Labs.

### The Ubunifu-Ansai relationship

Ubunifu Village builds the substrate.
Ansai Technologies builds products on the substrate.
Ansai generates the revenue that funds Ubunifu's work.
The data Ansai collects trains the models Ubunifu AI develops.
The hardware Ubunifu Infra builds hosts the cloud
Ubunifu Cloud operates.
Nothing graduates from Ubunifu into Ansai.
The relationship is permanent interdependence, not a pipeline.

### Product line cost centres

Each product line operates as its own cost centre
from its first transaction. Revenue and costs are
attributed per product line from day one.
This is a bookkeeping discipline, not a legal structure.
It means that if a product line warrants its own legal
entity, the financial history is already clean and separable.

### The holding company trigger

Ansai moves to a formal holding structure when external
investors want equity specifically in one product line
rather than Ansai broadly, or when multiple product lines
have genuinely separate capital structures, team sizes,
or regulatory environments.
Until that trigger is met, one legal entity is correct.

## 07 — The non-negotiables

These are the identity expressions of the foundational
convictions in GWP-001 Section 4.
When these and GWP-001 appear to conflict, GWP-001 governs.

Every person who joins any Ansai product team must
understand and agree to these before beginning work.

**We build for Africa.**
Not for a market segment — for the people inside every
institution that shapes African life.
An Ansai product that does not serve Africans directly
is not an Ansai product.

**We build infrastructure, not apps.**
Every product line must be depended upon daily,
not used occasionally.
If a school, farm, or clinic could easily stop using it,
we have not built infrastructure.
The test: would they feel it if it went offline for a week?

**We build with Utu.**
Full awareness of the human being on the other side
of every screen. Every product decision is made with
the specific person it affects in mind — not a user
persona, a named human being.

**Data belongs to the institution.**
Every school, farm, or clinic that uses an Ansai product
owns its data. Ansai holds it in trust, not in ownership.
This is non-negotiable across every product line
and every jurisdiction.

**The enabling stack is built from the products outward.**
No layer of the enabling stack is built before the
product lines that justify it exist.
EduManage earns the right to the data intelligence layer.
The data intelligence layer earns the right to the
developer platform. Sequence is discipline.

**Earn our welcome before claiming a place.**
In African institutional markets, trust is built through
presence — not pitches, not promises, not demos alone.
Where conditions allow, we show up before we pitch.
We listen before we build. We return with what we heard.

This is not always perfectly achievable. Markets move,
timelines compress, opportunities require faster action
than ideal. The principle does not demand perfection —
it demands direction. When forced to choose between
a faster approach and a presence-first approach,
we lean toward presence. Always.
